Research

I use causal inference and data-rich empirical methods to study policy-relevant questions in health, crime, and the economics of AI.

Job market paper

Decriminalization and Its Discontents

Crime and health effects of drug-policy reforms in Washington and Oregon. This project evaluates the effects of Washington's Blake decision and Oregon Measure 110 using linked administrative data and modern difference-in-differences methods.

Under review

Showing the Person Beats Describing the Person in LLM Synthetic Personas

An experimental study of whether LLM-based synthetic personas can replicate human decision-making, and how behavioral histories improve their predictive performance.

Policy research

Health and labor-market outcomes

Collaborative policy research on the economic effects of adolescent mental health on labor-market outcomes, with attention to causal design, survey-weighted estimation, and policy interpretation.
